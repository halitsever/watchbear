How to host an online watch party that actually stays in sync

Everyone presses play at once, and five minutes later someone is a scene ahead. Here's what you need, how to set up a synced room, and what to do when something goes wrong.

Oct 26, 20266 min read

Most online watch parties start with a countdown in the group chat. "Ok, 3, 2, 1, play." One person's video buffers, another paused to grab a drink, and twenty minutes later the chat is full of jokes about a scene half the group hasn't reached yet.

A watch party tool takes care of the timing. It keeps every player in the room on the same second, so when one person pauses or skips, everyone's video does the same. Everything else works like hosting at home: pick something good, tell people when to show up, and have snacks ready.

What everyone needs

Every guest needs Chrome on a laptop or desktop, since Watchbear is a Chrome extension. People join a room by signing in with their Google account, so nobody has to create a new password.

Everyone also needs access to the video itself. Each person plays it in their own browser, which costs nothing on YouTube or Vimeo. On a paid streaming service, every guest needs their own login. A sync tool shares the playback position between players. It never sends the video to anyone.

Why not just share your screen?

Screen sharing on Zoom, Discord or Google Meet is what most groups try first, and it works in a pinch. The trouble is that the film goes through a call codec built for faces. Fast scenes turn to smudges, frames drop, and the sound can slip out of step with the picture. The host's upload connection carries the stream for the whole group, and guests can't pause or rewind for themselves.

With a sync extension, each person's player streams at full quality straight from the site. The only thing traveling between you is the playback state: playing or paused, and at which second. If one guest has a slow connection, it doesn't drag everyone else down with them.

How to host a watch party with Watchbear

1. Install the extension

Add Watchbear from the Chrome Web Store and pin it to your toolbar so the bear icon is easy to find.

2. Open the video

Go to the page where the video plays, like a YouTube video or a Vimeo link. Watchbear looks for the main video on the page, so open the video itself rather than a feed or a homepage full of previews.

3. Click the bear

The Watchbear side panel opens next to the video with a room ready. Sign in with Google if it asks.

4. Send the invite link

Copy the invite link or the short room code and drop it in your group chat. The link opens the same video for your friends and puts them straight into the room. If someone doesn't have Watchbear yet, the invite page sends them to install it first.

5. Press play once everyone's in

Wait until the whole group has joined, then press play. From then on anyone can play, pause or skip, and every screen in the room follows.

6. Talk while you watch

Chat lives in the side panel, so messages never cover the film, and emoji reactions are one click away for jump scares and plot twists. Voice and video chat are built in as well. They start switched off, so nobody is on camera until they decide to be.

Tip: send the link ten minutes before the start time. First-time guests need a minute to install the extension and sign in, and it's better if that happens before the opening scene.

Tips for a smoother night

  • Post the start time in everyone's time zone. "9pm" doesn't mean much to a group spread across three countries.
  • Check availability before you choose. Streaming catalogs differ by country, so a film one friend can watch may be missing for another.
  • Agree on a pause rule. Since anyone can pause, ask people to say "brb" in the chat before they stop the film for a snack.
  • Wear headphones if you're on voice. Otherwise your mic picks up the film's audio and everyone hears it twice.
  • A room holds up to 50 people, so a club or a whole class fits. In a group that size, keep voice for a few people and let everyone else use the chat.

When something goes wrong

Someone drifts out of sync

Skip back a few seconds. A skip moves every player in the room, so everyone lands on the same frame again. If one person is still off, have them reload the page and open the invite link again.

Watchbear doesn't find the video

Make sure you're on the video's own page and the player has finished loading. Pages that show several small previews make it harder to tell which video is the main one.

One person gets an ad

On YouTube, guests without Premium can get ads at different moments. Pause until they're back, then carry on.

A streaming service won't cooperate

Watchbear works on YouTube, Vimeo and most sites with a standard video player. Some streaming services use custom, DRM-protected players, and support for those varies by site.
