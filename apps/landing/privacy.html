Privacy policy

Last updated: September 8, 2026

The short version

  • Starting or joining a watch party requires signing in with Google.
  • When you sign in, we store your Google email, name, and profile picture so your account works across devices. You can delete it any time by emailing us.
  • We record how the extension is used (for example that a party was started or an invite link was copied) on our own server, to improve Watchbear and to see which marketing brings people in. No third-party trackers, and we never sell or rent your data.
  • Chat messages, page URLs, page titles and the video itself are never part of those records. Watch-party data lives only in the server's memory while the room is open, and is gone the moment the room ends.

Who this policy covers

This policy explains how the Watchbear browser extension, the Watchbear server at watchbear-server.deepfeld.com, and this website (watchbear.deepfeld.com) handle information (together, the "Service"). It applies to the extension published on the Chrome Web Store.

What Watchbear does not collect

Watchbear is built to know as little about you as possible. We do not collect:

  • Passwords. Signing in goes through Google, so we never see or store one.
  • Your browsing history, the content of the pages you visit, or your search queries. Usage records keep only the domain of the video site (such as youtube.com), never the page.
  • The text of your chat messages. They are relayed to your room and never written down.
  • Cookies, login sessions, or credentials from the websites you watch on.
  • IP addresses or device fingerprints. The server may note a two-letter country code for a request (see "Product analytics"), but does not store the address it came from.
  • Any data for sale to third parties, or for advertising to you.

Information stored on your device

The following is saved using your browser's local extension storage (chrome.storage.local):

  • Display name and bear color: the nickname and avatar color you pick, so you look consistent in rooms.
  • Current room state: the active room code and which tab is leading playback, so a room survives a popup close or reload.
  • Sign-in session: if you sign in, your login token and basic Google profile (email, name, picture) are cached here so you stay signed in. Signing out removes it.
  • Analytics identifiers: a random device identifier created when the extension is first used, a usage-session identifier that resets after 30 idle minutes, and a short queue of usage events waiting to be sent to our server.
  • Campaign and invite details: if you arrived through an ad, a link with campaign parameters, or a friend's invite link, the campaign parameters, referring site, and invite identifier are kept so the extension can tell us what brought you here (see "Product analytics").

You can clear all of this at any time by removing the extension or clearing its storage.

Accounts and signing in

On the default public server, signing in with Google is required to start or join a watch party and to pick a custom display name, bear color, or avatar.

Signing in goes through Google, and we never see your Google password. Once Google confirms who you are, the server saves a small account record so your profile works across devices: a Google account identifier, your email, your name, your profile picture URL, and the dates the record was created and last updated. To keep you signed in, the server hands the extension a login token that expires after 30 days. There is no password on file, and no billing or subscription data.

When you sign in, the extension also sends the device identifier and any campaign or invite details it holds (see "Product analytics"). The server attaches them to your account so we can tell which campaign or which friend's invite brought you, and it records whether the sign-in created a new account or matched an existing one.

Your account record stays in the server's database until you ask us to remove it. To delete it, email watchbear@deepfeld.com and we'll erase your record and anything tied to it, including usage records.

Information shared during a watch party

When you start or join a room, the extension connects to the sync server over a real-time (WebSocket) connection and shares only what's needed to keep everyone in sync:

  • Your chosen display name and bear color.
  • The room code you're in.
  • The video you're watching: the page URL, page title, and a derived identifier used to confirm everyone is on the same video.
  • The playback position (current time) and play/pause state.
  • Chat messages, typing indicators, and emoji reactions you send in the room.

This information is relayed to the other members of your room so the experience stays in sync. It is only shared while you are in a room, and only with that room. The server keeps none of it once the room ends; what it does keep about the party is described next.

Product analytics

Watchbear records how the extension is used so we can improve it, find bugs, and understand which marketing brings people who keep using it. These records are stored on our own server only. There are no third-party analytics or advertising SDKs, and nothing is shared with ad networks.

What is recorded. Usage events such as:

  • Opening the popup, side panel, or setup page, and which setup step you reached.
  • Starting, joining, and leaving a party, and how many people were in it.
  • Copying an invite link, opening one, and joining through it.
  • How long a party had video playing and how much of it each member was present for, in seconds.
  • That a chat message or reaction was sent, that voice chat was joined, and which reaction emoji was used. The text of messages is never recorded.
  • Errors the extension ran into, described in a few words without page addresses.

Each event carries the extension version, browser name and version, operating system, browser language, the random device identifier, the usage-session identifier, the room code if you were in a room, and the domain of the video site (for example youtube.com or netflix.com). When a request reaches our server through a hosting provider that supplies one, a two-letter country code is stored with it; the IP address itself is not.

Never recorded: chat text, page URLs, page titles, the video being played, your Google profile, passwords, login tokens, or cookies.

Marketing attribution. When you reach this website from an ad or a shared link, the site keeps the campaign parameters in the address (such as utm_source, fbclid, or gclid), the referring site, and a random visitor identifier in your browser's local storage. If you then install the extension, it reads those values from the open Watchbear tab so we know which campaign led to the install. Invite links carry an invite identifier, so when a friend joins through your link we can count it and link the two accounts as inviter and invitee. The first campaign that brought you is kept unchanged; later visits only update the most recent one.

Usage records are kept on the server and tied to your device identifier and, after you sign in, to your account. Deleting your account deletes them. We may keep aggregate statistics that cannot identify anyone.

How long data is kept

Room data lives only in memory, only while the room is live. When the last person leaves a room, or whenever the server restarts, all of that room's data (members, chat, reactions, playback state, the video being watched) is permanently discarded. We do not keep chat logs, history, or backups, and the server does not log your IP address.

Two things are written to disk: your account record, if you sign in, and the usage records described under "Product analytics" (including that a party or invite happened, but not what was said or watched). Both are kept until you ask us to delete your account.

What the extension can access on web pages

To sync playback, Watchbear's content script reads only what it needs from the page you're watching: the main video element's current time, play/pause state, and duration, plus the page's title and URL so the room knows which video you're on. It does not read other page content, form inputs, cookies, or stored site data. On watchbear.deepfeld.com only, it also reads the campaign parameters in the address and the visitor identifier this site stored, and right after installation it checks whether a Watchbear tab is open to read the same values.

Permissions we request, and why

  • storage: to save your display name, bear color, current room, sign-in session, and the analytics identifiers and event queue on your device.
  • activeTab and scripting: to detect and sync the video in the tab you're actively watching, and to read campaign details from an open Watchbear tab at install time.
  • sidePanel: to show the chat and reactions panel next to your video.
  • identity: to sign you in with Google through the browser's standard sign-in flow.
  • tabGroups: to put the tab hosting your party in a small labeled tab group so you can spot it, and to remove that group when you leave.
  • Host access to websites (<all_urls>): because a watch party can happen on any site with a video player; the extension only reads video state and the page title/URL, and only acts when you're in a room.

Third parties and tracking

Watchbear contains no advertising and no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs. The usage records described above go only to our own server. The extension talks to two places: the Watchbear server for watch parties and usage records, Google to verify your sign-in, and nowhere else. This website (watchbear.deepfeld.com) loads fonts from Google Fonts and an icon script from iconify.design to render its pages; the extension itself does not.

Children's privacy

Watchbear is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has signed in and created an account, email us and we'll delete it.
