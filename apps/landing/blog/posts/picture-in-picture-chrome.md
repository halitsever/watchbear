Picture-in-picture takes a video out of its tab and floats it in a small window that stays on top of everything else. You can keep a talk, a match or a tutorial in a corner while you write an email, switch tabs or use another app entirely. Chrome on a computer supports it without installing anything.

The steps below come from Google's help page, [Control your music, videos, and more](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/9692215), checked on 28 November 2026, plus Google's own picture-in-picture extension.

## Way 1: the Media control button

This is the method Google documents, and it works on most sites.

1. Play a video in a tab.
2. At the top right of Chrome, next to the address bar, select **Media control**. The button appears while something is playing.
3. Select **Enter picture-in-picture**.

The video jumps into a floating window. Drag it to move it. You can now switch tabs or apps, and the video keeps playing on top. Close the floating window when you want the video back in its tab.

The same Media control button also lets you pause and skip without going back to the tab, which is handy on its own.

## Way 2: the right-click menu

Chrome's own right-click menu on a video has a **Picture in picture** option. Some sites replace the right-click menu with their own. On YouTube, for example, the first right-click opens YouTube's menu, and a second right-click on top of it shows Chrome's menu, where you'll find the option.

## Way 3: Google's Picture-in-Picture extension

If you use it often, Google publishes a small [Picture-in-Picture Extension](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/picture-in-picture-extens/hkgfoiooedgoejojocmhlaklaeopbecg) on the Chrome Web Store. Its listing describes it as a way to watch videos in a floating window that stays on top of other windows, adds a keyboard shortcut, **Alt + P** (**Option + P** on macOS), and links to its source code on GitHub. Click its toolbar icon or press the shortcut on a page with a video.

## Automatic picture-in-picture

Chrome can also move a video into a floating window by itself when you switch away from the tab. Google's page lists the conditions:

- It only works on sites whose developers enabled it.
- The first time, Chrome asks: **Allow this time**, **Allow on every visit** or **Don't allow**.
- It is off by default in Incognito mode.

To change your answer later:

1. Open **More**, then **Settings**.
2. Go to **Privacy and security**, then **Site settings**.
3. Under **Permissions**, open **Additional permission settings**, then **Automatic picture-in-picture**.
4. Choose whether sites can enter picture-in-picture automatically.

You can also switch it for a single site from the **Page info** icon on the left of the address bar.

## When the option is missing

- **Nothing is playing.** The Media control button only appears while a tab is playing sound or video. Press play first.
- **The site blocks it.** Some players turn picture-in-picture off, and Google's page notes that automatic picture-in-picture depends on the site's developer too.
- **Chrome is out of date.** Open **More**, **Help**, **About Google Chrome** to update it.

If video in the floating window stutters or shows a black frame, the cause is usually the same as in a normal tab. Our guide to [hardware acceleration in Chrome](chrome-hardware-acceleration.html) shows how to check it.

## Picture-in-picture and watching together

Picture-in-picture is for watching alone while you do something else. When you watch with friends, the opposite usually works better: keep the film big in its tab and put the conversation next to it. That is how Watchbear is built. The film plays in its own tab on Netflix, YouTube, Vimeo or most standard video players, and the chat, reactions and video call sit in [Chrome's side panel](chrome-side-panel.html) beside it, so nothing covers the picture.
