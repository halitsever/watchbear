Hyperbeam Watch Party takes an unusual approach to watching together. Instead of syncing each person's video, it runs one web browser on its own servers and streams that browser to everyone in the room. Anyone can be handed control, and whatever the browser shows, everyone sees.

That design is why people like it, and also why some go looking for alternatives. This guide explains how Hyperbeam works, what changed around it, and five alternatives that solve the same problem in different ways. Facts about each service come from its own website, checked on 28 November 2026.

## How Hyperbeam works

According to [Hyperbeam Watch Party's own page](https://watch.hyperbeam.com/), it is a multiplayer web browser shared with everyone in your virtual room. Because the browser runs on Hyperbeam's servers, its page says you don't need a fast connection to get a good stream. The feature list includes website and video playlists, passing control with a click, saved sites, chat with emojis and GIFs, audio and video calls, private rooms and custom invite links.

Two things we noticed when we checked:

- Hyperbeam's main site, hyperbeam.com, now presents the company's Virtual Computer API for developers, and sends people looking for the watch party to watch.hyperbeam.com.
- The watch party site's blog was last updated in 2022, and the help center address linked from it, hyperbeam.support, did not resolve when we tried it.

None of that means the service is gone. It does mean that if you need help or a fix, it may be slow to come, which is a fair reason to know the alternatives.

## Five alternatives, by how they work

Watch party tools fall into four families. Knowing which family a tool belongs to tells you most of what you need.

| Tool | How it works | Streaming services | Accounts | Phones |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hyperbeam | One cloud browser streamed to everyone | Whatever the shared browser can open | One person signs in inside the shared browser | Lists a download and a browser version |
| Kosmi | Rooms with shared browsing, screen sharing and built-in apps | Says it works with Netflix, YouTube and other services | No sign-up needed to join | Mobile app, desktop or tablet recommended |
| Watch2Gether | A shared player on its website | YouTube, Vimeo, Dailymotion, SoundCloud; Netflix and others through its W2gSync extension | No account needed for temporary rooms | Works in mobile browsers |
| Teleparty | Browser extension that syncs each person's player | A named list: Netflix, Disney+, Hulu, Prime Video, YouTube, HBO Max free; more with Premium | Each person uses their own streaming account | Apps for some services |
| Discord | Watch Together activity or Go Live screen sharing | Watch Together is YouTube only; Go Live shows your screen | Discord account | Discord apps |
| Watchbear | Chrome extension that syncs each person's player | Netflix with dedicated handling, YouTube, Vimeo, most standard video players | Google sign-in, plus each person's own streaming account | No, Chrome on a computer |

### Kosmi: the closest to Hyperbeam

[Kosmi](https://kosmi.io/) describes itself as a free watch party platform for movie nights, game nights and hangouts. Like Hyperbeam it runs in the browser with no download, and it builds shared browsing, screen sharing, link sharing and file sharing into each room, along with games, music, voice, video and text chat. Rooms can be customised, there are public rooms in a lobby, and a mobile app exists, though Kosmi recommends a desktop or tablet. Its page says it is free, with an optional Premium.

If what you liked about Hyperbeam was hanging out in a room and passing things around, Kosmi is the nearest match.

### Watch2Gether: fastest for YouTube links

[Watch2Gether](https://w2g.tv/) gives your group a room with a shared player. Paste a YouTube, Vimeo, Dailymotion or SoundCloud link and everyone sees it, no install and no account needed for a temporary room. For services its player can't embed, like Netflix, it points you to its browser extension and W2gSync. The free version shows banner ads, and webcams work for rooms of up to 10 people. Our [Watch2Gether comparison](../watch2gether-alternative) has the details.

### Teleparty: the streaming-service specialist

Teleparty is an extension that keeps each person's own player in sync on a named list of services. Its free plan covers Netflix, Disney+, Hulu, Prime Video, YouTube and HBO Max, and Premium adds more services plus your own camera and microphone. Parties can be very large. We cover its plans in [is Teleparty free?](is-teleparty-free.html) and compare it with Watchbear on the [Teleparty alternative page](../teleparty-alternative.html).

### Discord: if your group already lives there

Discord's Watch Together activity plays YouTube inside a voice call, and Go Live streams your screen to the channel. Both are free and need nothing new if your friends are on Discord already, but Watch Together is YouTube only and has a time limit, and screen sharing a streaming service often shows a black screen. Our [Discord watch party guide](discord-watch-party.html) compares the options.

### Watchbear: synced players with free video chat

Watchbear, which we make, is a Chrome extension. Instead of streaming one browser to everyone, it keeps everyone's own player on the same second: you open the film on Netflix, YouTube, Vimeo or most sites with a standard video player, start a room, and send the invite link. Chat, reactions and voice and video chat for up to 8 people sit in Chrome's side panel next to the film, in rooms of up to 50, and none of it costs anything.

The trade-offs are clear: everyone needs Chrome on a computer, a Google sign-in, and their own access to the video, such as their own Netflix account. There is no phone version.

## Cloud browser or synced players?

The biggest choice is between Hyperbeam's model and the sync model, and each has a real advantage.

**A shared cloud browser** means only one person needs to open the site. Friends don't install anything or sign in anywhere, and slow connections matter less because the heavy lifting happens on the server. The catch is that everyone watches a stream of one browser, and whoever signs in to a site inside it is using their account on someone else's machine, in front of the whole room.

**Synced players** mean each person watches the original site in their own browser, at the quality their own connection and account allow, and only play, pause and the position travel between you. The catch is setup: everyone installs the same extension and needs their own access to the video.

For a casual night of clips and browsing together, a shared browser or Kosmi-style room is hard to beat. For a full film on a streaming service with friends who each have an account, synced players are the safer bet.

## Questions to ask before you switch

1. **Where are your friends?** If anyone joins from a phone, rule out Chrome-only tools.
2. **What are you watching?** YouTube clips, a film on Netflix, a file on your computer and a live game all point to different tools.
3. **Does everyone have access?** Sync tools need everyone to have the video. Shared browsers and screen sharing don't.
4. **Do you want to see each other?** Check whether video chat is free for everyone or only for paying members.

If the answers are "computers, Netflix or YouTube, yes, and yes", [try Watchbear](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/watchbear-watch-together/ldegfikaldilbcpgmiopdnnhpkpcnepn). If not, one of the others above will fit better, and that is fine.
