Long-distance movie night: 10 ideas that feel like hanging out

Pressing play at the same moment is the easy part. These ideas are for the rest of the evening, for couples and friends who would normally share a couch.

Oct 26, 20266 min read

A movie night with someone in another city can end up feeling like two people watching TV with a phone call running in the background. The couch version is fun because of everything around the film: arguing over what to pick, stealing each other's snacks, talking over the slow parts. Most of that carries over to a long-distance night if you plan for it a little.

10 ideas to try

1. Eat the same thing

Agree on the menu before the night. Order the same takeout, buy the same frozen pizza, or cook one recipe together on a video call. Comparing whose version came out better is a good opening act.

2. Take turns picking, with one veto each

Alternate who chooses the film. The other person gets one veto a month, which they can spend on anything, so nobody sits through a third superhero movie in a row against their will.

3. Rewatch something from your childhood

Each of you picks a film you loved as a kid and haven't seen since. You'll learn something about each other, and you'll find out which ones held up and which ones really, really didn't.

4. Give the month a theme

One director's films in release order. One decade. Films from a country neither of you has been to. A theme also saves you the forty minutes of scrolling through menus before you can start.

5. Original versus remake

Watch the original one week and the remake the next, then argue about which one was better. Bonus points if you each grew up with a different version.

6. Bad movie night

Find a film with famously terrible reviews and make bingo cards before you start: a slow clap, a villain monologue, a twist you saw coming in minute four. Whoever fills a row first picks next week's film.

7. Start a series together

Pick a show neither of you has seen and agree on one rule: no watching ahead alone. It gives you a standing date every week, and something to text each other about between episodes.

8. A documentary and a debrief

Watch a documentary, then keep the call going for twenty minutes afterwards to talk about it. Documentaries tend to leave you with something to disagree about, which makes for a better conversation than "yeah, that was good."

9. A YouTube music video round

Each of you queues up three songs, the host plays them in turn, and you rate each one in the chat. It works as a warm-up before a film, or as the whole night when you can't agree on a movie. Our guide to watching YouTube together covers the setup.

10. Cameras on for dinner, off for the film

Eat together on video first, the way you'd sit at a table. When the film starts, switch the cameras off so you're watching the movie instead of each other, and keep voice on for the commentary.

Getting the logistics out of the way

Time zones are usually the hardest part. When it's evening for one of you and two in the morning for the other, a weekend works better: breakfast with a film for one person, a late night for the other. Put it in a shared calendar so it doesn't get bumped.
