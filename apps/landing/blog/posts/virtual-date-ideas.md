A good date is mostly about doing something together. When you are in different cities or different countries, the video call takes care of being together. The ideas below take care of the doing.

They are grouped by mood, from quiet nights to competitive ones. None of them needs special equipment beyond a computer or phone with a camera. A few need both of you to have the same thing on hand, which is part of the fun.

## Before the date: set the scene

A few small things make a video date feel different from a normal call:

- **Book it.** Agree on a day and time in advance, as you would for a dinner out. If you are in different time zones, say both local times in the invite.
- **Change the setting.** Sit somewhere other than your desk. Light a candle, use a lamp instead of the ceiling light, put the laptop at eye level.
- **Use headphones** if you will play music or a film, so the sound from your speakers doesn't echo back into the call.
- **Plan one activity.** Not a schedule, just one thing to do. Conversation flows better when it has something to come back to.

## Food and drink

### 1. Cook the same recipe

Pick a recipe together, both buy the ingredients, and cook at the same time with the call open in the kitchen. Comparing results is half the date.

### 2. Order each other dinner

If you live in places with delivery, each of you orders a surprise meal for the other. You find out what you're eating when the doorbell rings.

### 3. A tasting night

Each of you picks the same few snacks, chocolates or teas, then taste them in the same order and rate them. Keep the scorecard for a rematch.

## Culture and learning

### 4. Take a museum tour

[Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/) has virtual tours and collections from museums around the world. One person shares their screen and drives, and you both choose which room to walk into next.

### 5. Read to each other

Pick a short book and take turns reading a chapter aloud. It works well as a ritual you return to every week, and it ends naturally when the chapter does.

### 6. Learn something new together

A language, a drawing technique, a dance, a card trick. Follow the same free video lesson and show each other what you managed at the end.

### 7. Swap playlists

Each of you makes a short playlist for the other, with a story behind every song. Play them in turn and tell the stories in between.

## Games

### 8. Play an online game

Plenty of browser and phone games are built for two players, from word games to drawing and guessing games. Choose one that leaves room to talk.

### 9. Run a quiz about each other

Write ten questions about yourself, from easy to obscure, and see how many your date gets right. Then swap.

### 10. Play a game you both own

If you both have the same video game, play together with the call open on a second device. Co-op games tend to be more fun on a date than competitive ones.

## Screens, on purpose

### 11. Watch a film in sync

A movie night is the classic virtual date, and it works best when both players stay on the same second and you can see each other's reaction. Each of you opens the film on your own account, and a sync tool keeps the players together. With [Watchbear](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/watchbear-watch-together/ldegfikaldilbcpgmiopdnnhpkpcnepn) in Chrome on a computer, either of you can pause, and the video call sits beside the film in the side panel. Our [long-distance movie night ideas](long-distance-movie-night-ideas.html) have ten ways to make that night feel special, and [how to watch movies together long distance](how-to-watch-movies-together-long-distance.html) covers the setup and time zones.

### 12. Start a series together

Pick a series neither of you has seen and watch an episode together each week. It gives you a standing date and something to talk about in between.

### 13. Plan a trip

Open a map and plan the next time you will see each other, or a dream trip you might never take. Save the places you both like to a shared list.

## Quiet nights

### 14. Watch the same sky

Step outside at the same moment for a sunset, a sunrise or the moon, even if it is a different time of day for each of you. A short one that is easy to fit into a busy week.

### 15. Do nothing together

Some of the best dates are just company: both of you reading, working on a hobby or tidying up with the call open. It is the closest thing to being in the same room.

## Picking the right one

- **First video date?** Choose something with built-in conversation, like the quiz or the playlist swap.
- **Tired after work?** A film, a series episode or a quiet night asks the least of you.
- **Want something to look forward to?** Start a weekly ritual: a book, a series or a recipe list.
- **Different time zones?** Short ideas like the shared sky or the tasting night fit into a narrow overlap.

Whatever you pick, the point is the same: something to share, not just something to say.
