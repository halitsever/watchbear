A Halloween watch party is the best excuse of the year to get scattered friends into the same room, even if that room is online. Horror is also the genre that suffers most when people watch out of sync. If your friend screams a full second before you see anything, the jump scare is gone.

Here's how to plan the night, keep every player on the same frame, and borrow a few traditions from in-person Halloween parties that still work over the internet.

## Plan the night early

Send the invite a few days ahead, not on the afternoon of the 31st. Halloween competes with parties, trick-or-treating and work events, and people need to know the time before they commit.

Put the start time in every time zone that's in the group. A 9pm start in one city can mean a 3am finish somewhere else, and a horror marathon at 3am is a different kind of scary. If the spread is wide, a weekend afternoon for one side can be a late night for the other.

Ask everyone to confirm they can stream the films before the night. Catalogs differ by country and by service, so one missing title can stall the whole lineup.

## Pick your Halloween movies by scare level

A good Halloween watch party lineup starts gentle and ends with the film people will talk about for a week. Sorting by scare level also helps when the group has one person who loves horror and one who watches through their fingers.

### Spooky, not scary

- Hocus Pocus
- Beetlejuice
- The Addams Family (1991)
- Coraline

These work as an opener while people are still arriving, or as the whole night for a mixed group with kids.

### Scary, but fun

- Scream (1996)
- Shaun of the Dead
- Get Out
- A Quiet Place

The middle slot is where the jump scares start. Scream and Shaun of the Dead also give the chat plenty to joke about.

### The main event

- Halloween (1978)
- The Shining
- The Conjuring
- Hereditary

Save these for last, when the snacks are gone and the lights are off.

If your group can't agree on a streaming service, Night of the Living Dead (1968) is in the public domain and easy to find on YouTube. Our guide to [watching YouTube together](watch-youtube-together.html) covers that setup.

## Set up a synced room

Horror depends on timing more than any other genre, so skip the "3, 2, 1, play" countdown. With a watch party extension, each person streams the film in their own browser and the extension keeps every player on the same second.

1. Install Watchbear from the [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/watchbear-watch-together/ldegfikaldilbcpgmiopdnnhpkpcnepn). Everyone needs Chrome on a laptop or desktop and a Google account.
2. Open the first film on the site you're watching it on.
3. Click the bear to open the side panel with a room ready.
4. Send the invite link to the group. It opens the same film and puts each person in the room.
5. Press play when everyone's in. Anyone can pause or skip, and every screen follows.

Watchbear works on YouTube, Vimeo, Netflix and most sites with a standard video player. Everyone needs their own access to whatever you're watching. If you're watching on Netflix, our [guide to watching Netflix together](how-to-watch-netflix-together-online.html) has a few service-specific tips.

For a marathon, ask everyone to set "Follow the host's video" to Auto in the panel. When the host opens the next film, the other tabs follow.

> **Tip:** if you're on voice, wear headphones. Speakers leak the film back into your microphone, and the quiet stretch before a jump scare is exactly when everyone will hear it.

## Halloween traditions that work online

### A costume check at the start

Ask everyone to dress up, even if it's just cat ears and eyeliner. Start the night with cameras on and give each costume a minute. Watchbear has voice and video chat built in, and cameras start off, so people switch on when they're ready. Our [watch party video chat guide](watch-party-with-video-chat.html) has more on running a call during a film.

### Jump scare bets

Before the film starts, everyone posts in the chat how many jump scares they expect. During the film, each scare gets an emoji reaction. Whoever guessed closest picks the next film.

### Horror bingo

Make bingo cards with the usual horror moments: the phone with no signal, the "let's split up" decision, the car that won't start, the fake-out scare with a cat. First person with a full row announces it in the chat. You'll find more ideas in our list of [watch party games](watch-party-games.html).

### Matching snacks

Agree on a snack theme in advance. Candy corn, a bowl of something blood-red, or the same takeout order. Showing off the bowls on camera before the film takes two minutes and makes the night feel shared.

### Lights off, phones down

Make one house rule: once the main event starts, lights off and phones face down. Horror works better in the dark, and it keeps people from scrolling through the slow build-up.

## A sample Halloween watch party schedule

- 8:00 Costume check on camera and snack show-and-tell
- 8:15 Spooky opener, something like Hocus Pocus or Beetlejuice
- 9:50 Short break and jump scare bets for the main film
- 10:00 The main event, lights off
- 11:45 Cameras back on to rank the films and argue about the ending

Adjust to your group. If half of them have to get up early, drop the opener and start with the scary one.
