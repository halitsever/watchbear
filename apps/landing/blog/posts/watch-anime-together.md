Watching anime with friends online has a rhythm of its own. Episodes run around twenty-odd minutes, so a night means switching episodes several times. One friend wants subtitles, another wants the dub. Someone wants to sing along to the opening and someone else wants to skip it. And the best moments are the ones you want to react to at the exact same second.

Here is how to set up an anime night that keeps everyone on the same frame for a whole season.

## Where you can watch anime together

A watch party extension doesn't send video to anyone. Each person plays the episode from wherever it's published, and the extension keeps those players in step. So the first question is where the show is, and whether everyone can open it there.

### Netflix

Netflix carries a lot of anime, and Watchbear has dedicated handling for Netflix's player, so skips and episode changes land on the right second. Everyone needs their own Netflix account, and the catalog differs by country, so check the show is there for everyone before the night.

### YouTube

Some anime distributors publish episodes on official YouTube channels, often limited to certain regions. Watchbear works on YouTube directly, and so does Discord's Watch Together activity if your group lives in a Discord server. If a video is blocked in one friend's country, nothing in the room can change that, so check the link with everyone first.

### Crunchyroll and other streaming services

Watchbear finds the main video on any page with a standard player, but it has no dedicated handling for Crunchyroll or other subscription services besides Netflix. Copy-protected players behave differently from site to site, so try it with one friend before you plan a group night around it.

For comparison, Teleparty lists Crunchyroll as a Premium service on its [support page](https://www.teleparty.com/support), at $6.59 a month or $3.99 a month billed yearly.

## How to set up an anime watch party

### 1. Install Watchbear

Everyone adds [Watchbear](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/watchbear-watch-together/ldegfikaldilbcpgmiopdnnhpkpcnepn) to Chrome on a computer and signs in with Google. Phones and tablets can't join.

### 2. Open the first episode and start a party

The host opens the episode, clicks the bear and chooses **Start a party with this video**. The room opens in Chrome's side panel next to the player.

### 3. Send the invite link

Paste the invite link in your group chat or Discord channel, or read out the room code. Friends who open the link land on the same episode inside the room.

### 4. Turn on Follow the host's video

This is the setting that matters most for anime. In the side panel, set **Follow the host's video** to Auto. When the host moves to the next episode, everyone's tab moves with them. Without it, every episode change means sending a new link.

### 5. Press play

Anyone in the room can pause or skip, and every player follows.

## Subs, dubs and the opening song

### Subtitles and audio are per person

Because each person plays the episode in their own player, audio and subtitle choices stay with them. One friend can watch with Japanese audio and English subtitles while another watches the dub. Only the position in the episode is shared. Before you rely on it, check that the subbed and dubbed versions are the same length on that service. If one has a different cut, the players will match on time but not on scene.

### Agree on openings and endings

A skip from anyone moves the whole room. That is what keeps you in sync, but it also means one impatient friend can skip the opening for everybody. Agree before episode one: skip, or sing along. Many groups watch the opening on the first episode of a session and skip it after that.

## Reacting without spoiling

The side panel has text chat and emoji reactions beside the episode, never over the subtitles. Reactions are the fast option for the moments where typing is too slow.

For bigger groups, voice and video chat are built in and free: up to 8 people on camera and microphone at once, in a room of up to 50. Cameras start off every time you join. If you're on voice, wear headphones, or your microphone will send the episode's audio back to everyone.

Two rules keep a long-running show fun:

- **Nobody watches ahead.** If someone has already seen the season, they can stay, but they don't react before the twist lands.
- **Keep theories in the chat, not on the mic.** Talking over dialogue is fine in a comedy and painful in a mystery.

## Picking up next week

A season takes several nights. Watchbear remembers the last thing you watched with a friend and where you stopped. The next time you open the popup, **Pick up where you left off** starts a new party at that spot with one tap and gives you a link to send. The position is stored only in your own browser.

If your group meets on Discord, our post on [Discord watch parties](discord-watch-party.html) covers how to keep the voice channel while the episodes play in sync.

## When something goes wrong

### One person drifts a few seconds behind

Skip back a few seconds. Every player follows, so you all land on the same frame. If it keeps happening, that person can reload the page and open the invite link again.

### The next episode didn't follow

Check that Follow the host's video is set to Auto for that person. If they ended up on a different episode anyway, they can open the invite link again to rejoin the host's video.

### The episode is blocked for one friend

That is a regional restriction or a subscription difference. Pick an episode or a service that everyone can open.
