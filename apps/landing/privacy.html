Privacy policy

Last updated: July 4, 2026

The short version

  • Starting or joining a watch party on the public server requires signing in with Google. Self-hosted servers can run without accounts.
  • When you sign in, we store your Google email, name, and profile picture so your account works across devices. You can delete it any time by emailing us.
  • We don't run analytics or trackers, and we never sell or rent your data.
  • Watch-party data (chat, playback position, the video you're on) lives only in the server's memory while the room is open, and is gone the moment the room ends.
  • Watchbear is open source and self-hostable, so you can run the whole thing yourself.

Who this policy covers

This policy explains how the Watchbear browser extension and the public Watchbear sync server (the "Service") handle information. It applies to the extension published on the Chrome Web Store and to the default public server at watchbear-server.deepfeld.com. If you connect the extension to a self-hosted server, the operator of that server is responsible for its data handling.

What Watchbear does not collect

Watchbear is built to know as little about you as possible. We do not collect:

  • Passwords. Signing in goes through Google, so we never see or store one.
  • Your browsing history, the content of the pages you visit, or your search queries.
  • Cookies, login sessions, or credentials from the websites you watch on.
  • IP addresses for logging or profiling, device fingerprints, or location data.
  • Any data for advertising, marketing, or sale to third parties.

Information stored on your device

The following is saved using your browser's local extension storage (chrome.storage.local). It stays on your device and is not sent to us unless you join a watch party:

  • Display name and bear color: the nickname and avatar color you pick, so you look consistent in rooms.
  • Sync server address: which server the extension connects to (the default public server, or your own).
  • Current room state: the active room code and which tab is leading playback, so a room survives a popup close or reload.
  • Sign-in session: if you sign in, your login token and basic Google profile (email, name, picture) are cached here so you stay signed in. Signing out removes it.

You can clear all of this at any time by removing the extension or clearing its storage.

Accounts and signing in

On the default public server, signing in with Google is required to start or join a watch party and to pick a custom display name, bear color, or avatar. Self-hosted servers can turn this requirement off, in which case guests join without an account.

Signing in goes through Google, and we never see your Google password. Once Google confirms who you are, the server saves a small account record so your profile works across devices: a Google account identifier, your email, your name, your profile picture URL, and the dates the record was created and last updated. To keep you signed in, the server hands the extension a login token that expires after 30 days. There is no password on file, and no billing or subscription data.

Your account record stays in the server's database until you ask us to remove it. To delete it, email watchbear@deepfeld.com and we'll erase your record and anything tied to it.

Information shared during a watch party

When you start or join a room, the extension connects to the sync server over a real-time (WebSocket) connection and shares only what's needed to keep everyone in sync:

  • Your chosen display name and bear color.
  • The room code you're in.
  • The video you're watching: the page URL, page title, and a derived identifier used to confirm everyone is on the same video.
  • The playback position (current time) and play/pause state.
  • Chat messages, typing indicators, and emoji reactions you send in the room.

This information is relayed to the other members of your room so the experience stays in sync. It is only shared while you are in a room, and only with that room.

How long data is kept

Room data lives only in memory, only while the room is live. When the last person leaves a room, or whenever the server restarts, all of that room's data (members, chat, reactions, playback state, the video being watched) is permanently discarded. We do not keep chat logs, history, or backups, and the server does not log your IP address.

Account data is the exception. If you sign in, your account record is saved in the server's database and kept until you ask us to delete it. That is the only thing Watchbear writes to disk about you.

What the extension can access on web pages

To sync playback, Watchbear's content script reads only what it needs from the page you're watching: the main video element's current time, play/pause state, and duration, plus the page's title and URL so the room knows which video you're on. It does not read other page content, form inputs, cookies, or stored site data.

Permissions we request, and why

  • storage: to save your display name, bear color, server address, and current room on your device.
  • activeTab and scripting: to detect and sync the video in the tab you're actively watching.
  • sidePanel: to show the chat and reactions panel next to your video.
  • Host access to websites (<all_urls>): because a watch party can happen on any site with a video player; the extension only reads video state and the page title/URL, and only acts when you're in a room.

Third parties and tracking

Watchbear contains no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party tracking SDKs. The extension's main network connection is the real-time link to the sync server you've selected (the public server by default). If you choose to sign in, it also talks to Google to verify your login and to our auth server to set up your session. This website (watchbear.deepfeld.com) loads fonts from Google Fonts to render its pages; the extension itself does not.

Self-hosting

Watchbear is open source and the server is fully self-hostable. You can point the extension at your own server from its settings, in which case your room data flows only to the server you control. See the Servers page for details.

Children's privacy

Watchbear is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has signed in and created an account, email us and we'll delete it.

Changes to this policy

If we change how Watchbear handles data, we'll update this page and revise the "Last updated" date above. Significant changes will be reflected here before they take effect.

Contact
