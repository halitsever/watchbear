Watchbear vs Watch2Gether: a shared player or an extension

Watch2Gether gives your group one room with a shared player on its website. Watchbear stays on the site you already watch on, such as Netflix or YouTube, and keeps everyone's own player in sync. Here is how the two compare, including where Watch2Gether is the better choice.

Why an extension instead of a shared player

In a Watch2Gether room, the video plays in the room's own player. That is quick for YouTube clips: paste a link, and nobody needs to install anything. For streaming services that cannot be embedded, Watch2Gether points you to its extension and W2gSync.

Watchbear starts from the other end. You open the video where it lives, on Netflix, YouTube, Vimeo or a course site, and click the bear. Everyone plays it on that site with their own account and full picture quality, and only play, pause and the position travel between you. Netflix has dedicated handling for its player. On other sites Watchbear looks for the main video on the page rather than checking a fixed list.

The trade-off is setup: everyone needs Chrome on a computer, the extension, and a Google sign-in.

Where Watch2Gether is stronger

  • No install, no account. A friend can open a room link in the browser and watch a YouTube video right away.
  • Phones. The website works in a mobile browser. Watchbear needs Chrome on a laptop or desktop.
  • Playlists. The room can search, queue and suggest videos together.
  • Bigger rooms and more browsers. Hundreds of people per room, and extensions for Firefox, Opera and Edge as well as Chrome.

Where Watchbear is stronger

  • You stay on the real site. Netflix, YouTube and Vimeo play in their own players, signed in to your own accounts.
  • Any page with a standard player. No list to check before you invite people.
  • A side panel instead of a new tab. Chat, reactions and cameras sit beside the video, never over it.
  • Peer to peer calls. Voice and video go directly between the people in the room. Our server only introduces them.

Common questions

What is a good alternative to Watch2Gether?

If your group watches on computers in Chrome and wants to stay on the original site, such as Netflix or YouTube, with free video chat, Watchbear is one option. If people join from phones, use Firefox, or want a shared playlist without installing anything, Watch2Gether itself is still the better fit.

Is Watch2Gether safe?

Watch2Gether's own documentation says anyone with a valid room link can join, and by default everyone in a room can pick videos, control the player and chat. Room owners can limit that under Permissions, appoint moderators and remove people, and the paid PRO plan can restrict a room to registered users. Share the link only with people you know.

Does Watch2Gether have an extension?

Yes. Watch2Gether offers browser extensions for Chrome, Firefox, Opera and Edge. Its documentation says the extension powers W2gSync, which syncs services like Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video that its built-in player cannot embed.

Do I need an account for Watchbear?

Yes. Everyone signs in with a Google account to start or join a Watchbear room. Watch2Gether lets you create a temporary room without an account, and its temporary rooms are deleted after 24 hours.

Which one has more people on video?

Watch2Gether allows up to 10 webcams in a room and turns webcams off when more than 10 people are in it. Watchbear allows up to 8 people on camera and microphone at once, in rooms of up to 50.
